Is the detector calibrated?

With 50 independent samples, two unrelated channels exceed |r| > 0.32 in 2.35% of windows. That is the textbook false-alarm rate. Every pair in the uncoupled arm is unrelated by construction, and there the detector fired in 8.76% of windows, 3.7× the nominal rate.

An agent drifting through a smooth, slowly changing field takes samples that repeat one another. The detector behaves as if each 50-sample window held about 30 independent samples. Its nominal significance should not be read literally.

Held-out recalibration

The uncoupled arms of seeds 42, 44, 46, 48 set the threshold that restores the nominal rate: |r| ≥ 0.42. Seeds 43, 45, 47, 49 were not used to choose it. On those seeds that threshold gives a false-alarm rate of 2.08%, and detects the injected couplings as follows:

Interpretation boundaries

  • Seeds, not agents or windows, are the independent units. Sign-flip p-values are exact over seeds and Holm-adjusted across the six pairs; they describe this generator and configuration only.
  • Agents and overlapping windows observing one field are dependent; their counts are never used as sample sizes.
  • The nominal false-alarm rate assumes independent samples. The uncoupled arm measures the real rate.
  • The uncoupled arm preserves field evolution, forcing noise, moderators, agent paths and all random draws.
  • This measures detector sensitivity to injected coupling, not causal discovery on real-world data.
  • Operator types are labels for one shared detector; lagged detection is not implemented.
